# sort_by_key + Reverse reads better than a hand-rolled comparator

When you want to sort a `Vec` by some field, the first reach is often `sort_by` with a closure that compares two items:

```rust
// descending sort by the second tuple field
pairs.sort_by(|a, b| b.1.cmp(&a.1));
```

This works but two things bug me. You have to remember the `b, a` swap to flip the order, and you're writing a comparator when really you just want to say "sort by this key."

`sort_by_key` + `std::cmp::Reverse` says exactly that:

```rust
pairs.sort_by_key(|&(_, score)| std::cmp::Reverse(score));
```

`Reverse` is a tiny newtype whose `Ord` impl flips comparisons. So you keep the natural "sort by this field" framing and just wrap the key to flip direction. No more squinting at `b.cmp(&a)` to figure out which way it sorts.

For ascending it's even shorter:

```rust
pairs.sort_by_key(|&(_, score)| score);
```

When the key is expensive to compute (allocates, hits I/O, etc.) prefer `sort_by_cached_key` — it computes each key once and reuses it, instead of recomputing during every comparison.
